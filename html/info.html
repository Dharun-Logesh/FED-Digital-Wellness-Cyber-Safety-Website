Understanding Digital Wellness

Digital wellness is about creating a healthy relationship with technology that enhances rather than detracts from your well-being.

Core Principles

Balance

Finding the right amount of screen time that supports your goals without overwhelming your life.

Mindfulness

Being intentional about when, why, and how you use technology.

Signs You Need Better Digital Wellness

  • You reach for your phone the moment you wake up.
  • You feel anxious when you don't have your phone.
  • You check your phone even when there are no notifications.
  • You find it hard to concentrate without checking social media.
  • You spend more time online than you planned — often without realizing it.

Don't Worry though, this is fixable!

Fixing digital wellness isn't about quitting technology — it's about using it smarter.

Tips on how you can adopt better Digital Wellness

Set Healthy Boundaries with Tech

Create tech-free zones and tech-free times in your day. For example, no phone use during meals, before bed, or while studying. Your brain needs breaks from constant stimulation — and so does your body.

Recommended timings to switch off your phones

🛌 30-60 mins before bedtime

📖 10-15 minutes before studying

🍴 Immediately before meals

Use Technology to Control Technology

Try setting up screen time limits on your phone — it's a simple way to stay in control and cut down on mindless scrolling.

Common Cyber Threats to Watch Out For

Phishing Attacks
  • Suspicious email addresses
  • Urgent action required messages
  • Grammar and spelling errors
  • Verify sender's identity
  • Don't click suspicious links
Malware Threats
  • Downloading from untrusted sources
  • Opening unknown attachments
  • Using outdated software
  • Keep software updated
  • Use antivirus protection
Password Insecurity
  • Using simple passwords
  • Reusing passwords
  • Sharing passwords
  • Use password manager
  • Enable 2FA when possible

Explaining different Cyber Attacks in simple terms

Malware attacks

Malware is bad software that sneaks into your device to cause harm, like viruses, ransomware (locks your files and asks for money), or spyware (spies on what you do).

  • Malwars steals the info and messes the system up
  • Download apps or files from trusted websites or official app stores
DDoS Attacks

Think of a DDoS attack like a traffic jam caused on purpose. Attackers flood a website with so many fake visitors that real users can't get through. It's like having thousands of people try to enter a shop at once.

  • Websites become slow or unavailable
  • Use DDoS protection services
Cross-Site Scripting (XSS)

Attackers insert bad code into a website, which then runs on visitors' browsers to steal information or hijack sessions.

  • Attackers inject malicious scripts into websites, and trick browser in running malicious code
  • Ensure you are using a trusted website that has invested alot into user safety in website browsing

Tips on how you can protect yourself online

Social Media Safety
  • 🔒 Make all social media accounts private
  • 👥 Only accept friend requests from people you know
  • 🚫 Never share personal information in public posts
  • ⏰ Set daily time limits for social media apps
Online Communication
  • 🤔 Think twice before posting anything online
  • ❌ Block and report cyberbullies immediately
  • 📸 Be careful with sharing photos/videos
  • 🎮 Use gaming chat only with known friends
Remember!

From surfing the web, to playing multiplayer video games. If you ever feel unsafe online or suspect a digital attack, inform a trusted adult immediately.

Community Support

Connect with others on similar journeys

Cyber Youth Singapore (CYS)

Over 1000 members strong, A not-for-profit, youth-led organization empowering young people to explore technology responsibly. They run bootcamps, workshops, and campaigns on topics like internet addiction, data privacy, cyberbullying, and media literacy.

TOUCH - DigitalMINDSET Intervention Programme

This 6-9 month intervention targets youths (12-21) struggling with excessive device use and emotional regulation. It focuses on controlling screen time, improving real-life responsibilities, and strengthening family dynamics through therapy and experiential group work.

Association of Information Security Professionals (AiSP)

AiSP engages youths and the public through interactive sessions—quizzes, games, and workshops—to raise awareness about cybersecurity, scams, and safe digital behavior. They also involve youth volunteers and institutions like polytechnics and universities to amplify outreach.
